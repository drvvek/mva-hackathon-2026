What this page does, and what it does not

How it works. Each excluded candidate carries the reasons written for it in Tables 17 to 21 of the report, each coded to a filter (f1 to f7), the cytotoxic firewall (FW), a mechanism-level verdict (M, the KILL-ABSOLUTE tier), the oncology scope (O) or another candidate-specific reason (X). A reason lapses when its filter is switched off or when the assumption it rests on is removed (W for the senolytic reasons under f5 and f6, Q for every f4 reason, O for the out-of-scope reason). A candidate re-opens only when no recorded reason remains. Reason X is never removed by a switch.

Assay inputs. The assay result (Table 12), the ISG readout (Table 11, assay 7) and the treatment phase (Table 11, assay 9) act only on ranked rows whose unlock conditions in Table 14 name them. On a ranked row, Opens means that an input meets one unlock condition and every other condition on the row still applies. Blocked means that an input fails one. Nothing is re-ranked. Off chemotherapy means remission is documented and the child is off cytotoxic drugs; the oncologist's agreement is not assumed.

Ranked, supportive and review rows are not excluded, so they have no reasons to remove. Their ✗ and ~ cells are named constraints that the report carries into unlock conditions. These rows show dependency notes written from Section 8, Table 12 and Tables 13 to 16 instead.

Reason source. "Report text" uses only the reasons the report writes out. Six of the nine KILL-ABSOLUTE rows carry a mechanism-level reason and no named filter. "Every ✗ in Figure 2" adds each failing cell of the figure, which is the more conservative reading. Both counts are shown in the summary.

Limits.

  • No re-ranking. Positions and unlock conditions are those of Table 14.
  • Figure 2 is not a decision function. Some ✗ cells on excluded rows are not reasons the report gives, and some reasons the report gives are not ✗ cells.
  • Gates f7a, f7b and f8: the report records a row-level consequence only for f7a (glycerol phenylbutyrate). Switching f7b or f8 off changes no row here.
  • Strict f4 treats renal clearance alone as a failure. It affects anakinra, ruxolitinib, baricitinib and imeglimin, the four agents the report records as passes with dose adjustment (Section 5.3).
  • Not switchable here: the diagnostic assumptions (A to E, G, J, K), the evidence-level assumptions (H, I, L, M) and the design rules (R, S). They change the question or the unlock conditions, not a filter. Assumption N is covered by the assay outcome selector and Assumption P by the treatment-phase selector.
  • RMS is not one of the 20 assumptions in Section 3. It stands for the one patient fact that Table 22 marks as non-transferable, the rhabdomyosarcoma on which the arimoclomol exclusion rests. Removing it re-opens arimoclomol and changes no other row. The preset General MVA1 family (Table 22) removes RMS and Assumption Q and sets the phase to off chemotherapy. It leaves Assumptions O, T and W, the cytotoxic firewall, the assay result and the ISG readout as in the report.
  • Gate f8 has no row-level effect here whatever the phase. Table 22 keeps it when the backbone lapses.
  • The tumour-directed use of vincristine, paclitaxel and dasatinib sits under Assumption O. Removing O removes the out-of-scope reason; the report does not assess those uses.
